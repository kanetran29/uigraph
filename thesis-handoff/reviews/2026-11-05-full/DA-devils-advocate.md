### Genuine strengths (one paragraph, only what is real)
The thesis discloses unusually much: 4.3.2/Table 4.5 lists every instrument correction, in-sample status is stated repeatedly, and the abstract itself flags that the first reference was "partly completed from the graph's output". ### Strongest Counter-Argument
A sceptical scholar would say the thesis does not measure static navigation extraction; it measures how far one author-and-AI team can push one tool on six applications while the answer key moves with the tool. Every decisive quantity is set by a choice the author made after seeing results. The "small deterministic tier" (159 edges, or 33 without the route-order assumption) is produced by demotion rules written in response to panels; the same extractor printed 1024 must edges at f17a302, 20 at the intermediate version, and would print 203 if guards were assumed to pass. That is a policy dial, not a property of applications. The loss ranking that "justifies" scope attribution depends on the order of the author's loss rules (Section 5.2 admits target-first reverses it on two applications). The "66.7 % recovery" is satisfied by assigning every site to every screen, which the thesis calls useless; the remaining evidence of quality is two hand-read references that apply the analysis's own relations, one completed from the graph and one built by the same AI assistant. The formal dataflow story (least fixpoint, monotone transfer) is explicitly not what runs: the evaluated pass has non-monotone fallbacks and stand-ins for two of four relations. The single out-of-sample comparison scores against a reference generated by the rival tool, was repaired after the fact, and its headline (94.1 % vs 10.2 %) is a relative recall over the union of two outputs, one of them 10x larger, labelled by the same AI from 12-pair samples, and reported on a subset that excludes the application where this tool's precision is 6.5 %. RQ4 (100 % detection) cannot fail by construction, and RQ5 ("the loss was the tool's") is true by definition for any bug the author fixed. The honest reading is a well-documented engineering log of one tool's development, not evidence about static analysis, scope attribution, or Angular applications at large.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. Policy-dial explanation of RQ1: the small must tier is an effect of conservative demotion rules (not of applications), and a calibrated alternative (guards assumed to pass, with sample-checking) would give a very different tier size.
2. The trivial baseline explains RQ3: assigning every site to the shell/all screens gives 100 % recovery; narrower-than-trivial width (1.7-18.7x) is the only quality signal and is unvalidated against rendered pages.
3. Recall in 5.3.8 is explained by output size: a larger over-approximating output scores high relative recall over a union, independent of method quality.
4. Dynamic crawling with credentials, or a hybrid, may recover the shells, guards and signed-in screens at lower design cost; this was not run on any subject.
5. A rule-based per-class join plus a few corpus fixes may be what the evaluated pass effectively is (7 extra sites).

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Consumers named in the motivation (test-generation tool users, coding-agent developers).
- Maintainers of the six applications and of Rima et al.'s tool (licence, fairness of the comparison, validation of reported dead links).
- An independent (non-author, non-assistant) annotator for loss classes, references and pairs.

### Unexamined Premise
That a route table plus syntactic construct counts is the right unit for "how much navigation is recovered": the denominator is defined by four syntactic patterns, so what the thesis calls recall is recall against its own parser's notion of a navigation.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The abstract's second sentence from the end ("The figures are in-sample") is honest but sits directly before the headline comparison; reordering is a presentation fix.
- The thesis's own Table 7.1 limits column is more candid than the abstract; the abstract should inherit it.
- If a case study on a proprietary app, or a run of a crawler on mifos, were added, M1/M9 would be answerable; if not, the claims should be narrowed to "this tool on these applications".
