### Summary Assessment
The thesis builds a static Angular transition-graph extractor and evaluates it on six pinned applications with five source-reading methods, four checks of the attribution analysis, and a mutation experiment. It adds a comparison with Rima et al.'s tool.

The evaluation is unusually candid. Every correction is listed, failed predictions are reported, and most numbers are generated from result files. The methodological problem is not concealment. It is that several headline claims rest on instruments that either cannot fail or are not independent of the author, the tool, or the same AI assistant. The RQ3 success criterion is monotone by construction. The two structural references apply the analysis's own relations, and one was completed from the graph's output. The mutation rate is close to guaranteed by construction. The must tier was shaped by red-team panels and then checked by readings made with the extractor's decisions in view.

The strongest new evidence, the comparison with Rima et al.'s tool, uses a relative-recall denominator (union of both tools' pairs) that favours the over-approximating tool. It samples at most 12 clustered pairs per stratum, and the headline subgroup ("without mifos") was formed after the results were read. The AI-assistant annotation is validated by the author alone.

I recommend Major Revision. The conclusion "usable but insufficient" is supportable. The abstract's quantitative comparative claims and the RQ3 and RQ4 answers need re-analysis, or reframing as descriptive results.

### Weaknesses
1. **W1 The RQ3 acceptance criterion is satisfied by construction, and scope accuracy is only tested against self-referential references**
   - **Problem**: RQ3 is "answered positively" if the recovered share rises, must conversion and recovered share do not fall, and precision does not fall. Attribution only adds edges to a baseline that is reproduced exactly with the pass off, so the first three conditions are guaranteed. The only informative condition is precision, and it is read for 97 of 159 must edges only. Whether the computed scopes are right (the actual contribution) is tested against two references that apply the analysis's own relations. One (realworld) was completed with nine header sites reported by the graph. Two repairs were guided by it, and the stated 100 % precision and recall are scored on the same reference. The alfresco reference's precision is 82.0 % and falls below the class join. The rendered-interface check judged 30 of 806 screens, on an earlier graph, and cannot test over-attribution (7 exact predictions, none refuted, "supports no rate"). The average site is attributed to 39.5 of 67 screens on k8sdash.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 50 "RQ3 is answered positively only if the recovered share and must conversion"`
   - **Why it matters**: A recovery of 66.7 % counts a construct as recovered when any edge witnesses it, regardless of whether the screen set is right. The thesis reports this as the main RQ3 result while stating that scope correctness is largely untested. The reader cannot tell a correct analysis from a wide one.
   - **Suggestion**: State RQ3 as a two-part question and drop the accept-if criterion. Part A is recovery, descriptive. Part B is scope accuracy, with a hypothesis that can fail. Build one reference that does not pass through the relations. Options are signing in to one large application and reading rendered links on a stratified sample of screens (the thesis lists this as its own cheapest strengthening in Section 6.5), or a blinded human annotation of a random sample of sites on a large application. Report the scope-width distribution next to recovery in the abstract.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: tool evaluation design
2. **W2 Relative recall in the head-to-head comparison is dominated by pair volume, and the sampling unit is clustered**
   - **Problem**: Recall is "the share of the real pairs either tool finds". The union is split into strata read at up to 12 pairs. On the five applications other than mifos this extractor produces 2084 pairs and the other tool 217, so relative recall (94.1 % against 10.2 %) mostly restates a 9-to-1 ratio of real-pair counts. Neither tool's absolute recall is measured. The unit is the pair (screen, screen), but a shell link in a scope ⊤ yields a pair for every screen, so many pairs share one site. Section 5.5.3 (p. 91) states that 39.4 % of all edges in the attributed graph come from one mifos call site. The 12 pairs per stratum are therefore far fewer independent observations than 12, and the Wilson interval, which assumes independent trials, is too narrow. The comparison also treats "real" as "a user on s can trigger a navigation to t". That counts a link visible on every screen as one real pair per screen, which rewards the tool that fans out.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table 5.19, p. 85, rows "Corpus without mifos": 217 vs 2084 pairs, recall 10.2 % vs 94.1 %`
   - **Why it matters**: The abstract states "finds 94.1 % of the real transitions either finds, against 10.2 %, at similar precision". It is the thesis's most striking comparative claim and the only one against an external tool. A reader will take it as recall. It is a share of a union that one tool inflates.
   - **Suggestion**: (a) Report counts of real pairs per tool, not only shares. (b) Add a site-level or (site, target) unit and compute intervals clustered by site, or sample sites and then pairs. (c) Obtain an absolute-recall estimate against a third reference neither tool generated: the e2e suites (19 and 22 pairs), or a blind hand-built sample from one small application. (d) Give an interval for the precision difference 72.6 % against 75.2 % before the abstract says "similar".
   - **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: estimation and pooled-reference designs
3. **W3 The headline subgroup and the mifos figures rest on post-hoc choices and tiny samples**
   - **Problem**: "Corpus without mifos" was added after reading, because mifos decides the pooled figures. The only separation of the recall bounds is in that group. The pooled corpus bounds span 79.6 %-99.4 % against 10.3 %-85.6 %, so they "do not rank the tools". The mifos estimates re-use earlier samples restricted to the pairs that remain once moves across mounts are dropped, so the strata behind the 6.5 % against 80.0 % precision figures are very small (the repository deviation record gives 7, 4 and 5 pairs). The declaration-to-route mapping was chosen after their output was read (the repository deviations say it can only help their tool, which is fair). Their tool was run before the preregistration was committed, and the thesis states the preregistration and logs were committed together in the e2e case. Eleven deviations over three passes are recorded in the repository.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 85 "its bounds separate only without mifos, a group formed after reading"`
   - **Why it matters**: The abstract states the without-mifos result and the mifos precision pair without saying the group is post hoc or how small the samples are. The preregistration is cited as a validity feature. With this many post-hoc changes it protects less than the text implies.
   - **Suggestion**: Put "post hoc" and the sample sizes in the abstract wherever those numbers appear. Lead the abstract with the per-application results and the pooled preregistered figure. Report mifos separately as a case where this extractor's precision is low, with its stratum sizes. If feasible, draw a fresh, larger mifos sample under a fixed rule and read it once.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: preregistration and post-hoc analysis
4. **W4 LLM-as-annotator validity is not established**
   - **Problem**: One assistant reads each pair for the head-to-head comparison and the Rima-subject comparison. A stronger model of the same assistant re-reads a subset (114 of 263 and changed 10, 8.8 %; 45 on the first comparison and changed 3). The author then re-read 41 and found 41 agreeing. The author is the tool's builder, and the text does not say the re-read was blind to the assistant's verdict or to which tool proposed the pair. No interrater statistic is reported, and 41/41 has an upper 95 % bound of about 7-9 % disagreement at best. The same assistant helped write the extractor, so the reader and the tool may share systematic readings. A later adversarial review found the reader counted a shell bar as shown on sign-in screens whose guard hides it, which is a mistake correlated with the extractor's own guard stand-in. The alfresco reference was built by the same assistant.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 86 "of the 41 I judged, 41 agreed with the reading"`
   - **Why it matters**: Every precision and recall figure in Table 5.19 and Table 5.18, and the alfresco reference in Table 5.14, depend on the readings being correct. Same-family agreement measures consistency only, as the thesis already says of the must-tier readings.
   - **Suggestion**: Have a second, human reader (a supervisor, colleague, or paid annotator) label a random sample of at least 60 pairs blind to tool and to the model's verdict, and report Cohen's kappa and the confusion matrix. Use a second model family for a cross-check. State in the thesis whether the author's re-read was blind. Propagate the observed error rate (about 9 % changed) into the intervals.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: annotation validity
5. **W5 The must tier is fitted and its check cannot detect the relevant failure**
   - **Problem**: The tier went from 1024 edges (26 failing) to 20 to 159, with the demotion rules written "in part in response to objections from red-team panels" run on the same graphs. The final tier is the one that survives those panels. The witness check compares only destinations, so by the thesis's own account it cannot find a construct that does not run every time, which is the main failure mode of a must edge. Of 159 edges, 125 come from one toolbar and 62 destinations are unread. The source reading of the tier was made by the assistant with the extractor's decisions in view, and the text states that neither it nor the mechanical check is "an independent proof of Claim 3.1". The route-order assumption underlies 126 edges. Claim 3.1 is therefore tested only by the author's own panels.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 94 "The demotion rules were written during development, in part in response to objections"`
   - **Why it matters**: The abstract's first "result that stands out" concerns the must tier. The thesis's only soundness claim is about this tier. The evidence that it holds is that the remaining defects are not yet found by the same people and tool that produced it.
   - **Suggestion**: Have a human reader who did not take part in the panels judge all 25 must constructs blind to the extractor's rationale, or run the 25 in a browser against a signed-in seeded backend and report whether the transition fires on each execution tested. Report the toolbar group as one finding with n = 1 construct, not 125 edges. For the 62 unread edges, either extend the checker to read them or state the cause in the abstract.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: soundness testing
6. **W6 RQ4 is answered by an experiment that is close to non-informative and was tuned on its misses**
   - **Problem**: Mutants are sampled only at constructs the graph already cites in their simplest readable form (a deleted routerLink is sampled only on a line an edge cites; a retarget only on a plain absolute string). The result is 67/67, or 45/45 without rename. The harness was corrected after reading its misses, and 46 of 73 mutants lie at the same location as the earlier run. The sample per operator and application is in single digits. Equivalent mutants are not discussed for the other three operators. The full candidate pool is 272, but only 67 are scored.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 55 "so the final run is not an independent test."`
   - **Why it matters**: The conclusion that the graph "responds to change" is stated for change impact analysis. The experiment cannot yield a miss except through a harness bug, so a null result is guaranteed. The honest finding is closer to a unit test of the witness mapping.
   - **Suggestion**: Reframe RQ4 as a sanity check on witness mapping, not a measure of sensitivity. If sensitivity is wanted, generate mutants from a syntactic operator over all source sites in scope (not from the graph), include the 272 − 67 mutants in the denominator, report which were not scored and why, and report a mutation score over all non-equivalent mutants with a Wilson interval.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: mutation testing methodology
7. **W7 RQ5 answer is partly tautological and single-annotator**
   - **Problem**: A loss is "the tool's" if the final version reads the cause. The author fixes each cause found, then reports it as the tool's. The set of causes is the ones discovered by the author's own reviews, so a never-discovered cause cannot appear. The attribution remainder (151 constructs) and the 96 dynamic-destination constructs are not classified as tool or application. The text says no measurement separates them. The cause classification has no second annotator (Table 6.1: "no second annotator for the loss classification").
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 11 "the information was in the source and the loss was the tool's."`
   - **Why it matters**: The abstract states "almost every cause of loss traced in the source is a construct a static reader can read, so the loss was the tool's". The share of constructs behind that statement is not quantified, and the denominator is the causes found, not the constructs lost.
   - **Suggestion**: Count constructs, not causes. Report what percentage of all lost constructs (including attribution remainder and dynamic) is attributed to each outcome, with the unclassified residual as its own row. Have a second reader classify a random sample of 30 residual constructs and report agreement. Fix the definition of "a static reader could read this" before the repair, and record it with the commit.
   - **Severity**: Major | **Confidence**: 3 — adjacent to core expertise; I did not re-trace causes in source
8. **W8 Pooled corpus figures are largely one application**
   - **Problem**: mifos holds 1343 of 1818 constructs and 75.7 % of in-scope sites. 218 of the 301 may recoveries and 94 % of its sites recovered drive the 66.7 % and 83.5 % figures. Without it the attribution recovery is 38.6 % and four of six applications recover under half. The six applications were selected with the tool in view, one rejection was on tool support, and the test-suite criterion was "not applied uniformly".
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 70 "Without mifos the share is 38.6 %, and 4 of the six applications recover under half."`
   - **Why it matters**: The thesis already says the figures should not be read as estimates for other applications. The pooled percentages still appear in the abstract and the conclusion table as if they described a population.
   - **Suggestion**: Report the median and range across applications as the primary summary and the pooled figure second. Use the pooled figures only with the application count named. In the abstract, replace "66.7 %" with the per-application range.
   - **Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: corpus validity
9. **W9 The e2e containment check has no chance baseline**
   - **Problem**: Containment is reported as hits among observed pairs (19/19, 19/22), with the graph holding 71 of 100 possible pairs on realworld and 162567 of 367236 (44 %) on mifos. A graph that placed pairs at random with the same density would be expected to contain about 13.5 of 19 and 9.7 of 22. The thesis does mention density, but does not compute the expectation or test the observed count against it.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 79 "so a hit on mifos discriminates less than it did"`
   - **Why it matters**: This is one of the very few checks whose reference is independent of the analysis's relations. Without a null the reader cannot judge how much weight 19/22 deserves.
   - **Suggestion**: Report a density-matched null (hypergeometric) and a stronger comparison: restrict to pairs where the target is not reachable from the source through the shell, and report hits there.
   - **Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise
10. **W10 Provenance of ordering claims and some figures cannot be verified from the artefacts**
   - **Problem**: The preregistration and the e2e logs were committed together, so the order "cannot be corroborated" by the archive. The oracle ran on a graph whose extractor commit was not recorded. realworld's e2e depends on an unpinned public backend. Superseded figures cannot be regenerated.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 62 "run on 29 and 30 October against a graph whose extractor commit was not recorded"`
   - **Why it matters**: Each item is disclosed, but together they weaken the claim that "every figure is generated". The runtime oracle is the thesis's external check for node soundness, and it is the part that cannot be re-run.
   - **Suggestion**: Re-run the oracle on the final commit (the thesis says it was only run once, 29-30 October). Record the digest of the measured graph. Pin or mock the realworld backend. Replace "preregistered" with "committed before scoring" where the ordering cannot be shown by timestamps.
   - **Severity**: Minor | **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: artefact evaluation
11. **W11 Ablation levels reimplement earlier work inside the new extractor**
   - **Problem**: The "rooted" and "outlet" levels stand for Oshima and Kishi and Rima et al., but are reimplementations in the same extractor and "not runs of either tool". The comparison with earlier work at those levels is therefore a comparison of the author's reading of their descriptions.
   - **Evidence Anchor**: `text: p. 76 "Both levels reimplement those mechanisms inside this extractor and are not runs of either tool."`
   - **Why it matters**: The abstract says "Unlike the mechanisms of earlier work, it places navigation inside services". For Rima et al. the tool was later run (airbus, 3 pairs), which supports the claim in one application. For Oshima and Kishi, no run exists.
   - **Suggestion**: Say "the levels as I read them" wherever those levels are called earlier work, and cross-reference the one real run. Do not generalise from airbus alone.

### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Hypotheses
Five RQs, each tied to one main measure (Table 4.2). RQ1 and RQ2 are descriptive and well specified. RQ3 and RQ4 are phrased as questions but evaluated with criteria that cannot fail (W1, W6). RQ5 mixes a classification with a causal statement (W7). Consider stating RQ3 and RQ4 as a measured quantity, not an acceptance rule.

#### Research Design
A tool-building thesis evaluated by the builder on the corpus used to build it. The design mitigates this with a frozen out-of-sample set, preregistration and a runtime oracle. The threat table (Table 6.1) is thorough. What the design lacks is an annotator independent of the builder and the assistant. The mitigation list is long, but each item is partly self-referential.

#### Sampling Strategy
Corpus: 10 screened, 6 retained, criteria not uniformly applied, one rejection on tool support (p. 45). The six are heterogeneous in size (14 to 909 routes), which helps, and mifos dominates pooled counts (W8). Sampling for readings: seeded, 12 per stratum, Wilson ends. The pair unit and the clustering are not handled (W2).

#### Data Collection
Constructs are found by an independent parser, whose independence has eroded (Table 4.5, three corrections made because it disagreed with the extractor). The harness, scorers and preregistrations are in the repository. Hand-compiled tables are marked.

#### Analysis Methods
No significance tests, so multiple-comparison problems do not arise. Interval reporting by Wilson ends is conservative and honest, but applies to clustered units. The post-hoc groups are labelled in the notes of the tables, not in the abstract (W3). Effect sizes are not needed for ratios. The cross-run paired comparison (51/51 against 50/51) is not informative.

#### Results Presentation
Tables are clear in content. The paper distinguishes constructs, edges and pairs carefully, but the three units differ by orders of magnitude (1818 constructs, 13830 edges, 162567 pairs) and the abstract mixes them. The unit should be named at every percentage in the abstract.

#### Reproducibility
Strong on the source-reading methods, weaker on the oracle and the realworld e2e (W10). The reproduction instructions (REPRODUCE.md) are cited. I did not re-run the artefacts.

#### Methodological Fallacies Detected
- Confirmation and fitting: the must tier and the harness were corrected after misses were read, then reported as passing (W5, W6).
- Circular reference: the realworld structural reference was completed from the graph's output; "100 % precision" is then scored against it (W1).
- Survivorship in the sample: the mutation sample is drawn from what the graph already represents (W6).
- Ecological or aggregation effect: pooled corpus percentages are mifos (W8). Not a Simpson reversal, but the pooled and non-mifos conclusions differ.
- Clustered sampling treated as independent (W2).
- Post-hoc subgroup selection (W3).

### Questions for Authors
1. How many of the 263 + 73 pairs read by the assistant were also read by a human blind to the assistant's verdict, and what is the agreement? Was your own re-read of 41 blind to the verdict and to the proposing tool?
2. If relative recall in Table 5.19 were computed over sites (or site-target pairs) instead of screen pairs, would 94.1 % against 10.2 % survive? What are the absolute counts of real pairs for each tool?
3. Which of the 25 must constructs have been judged by someone who took no part in the red-team panels, and without seeing the extractor's rationale?
4. What would the mutation score be if mutants were drawn from all syntactic sites of the four operators (not from sites the graph already cites), with equivalents removed?
5. What is the expected number of e2e hits under a density-matched null on realworld and mifos?

### Minor Issues
- Abstract (p. 1) is a full page and mixes constructs, edges and pairs without naming the unit at each percentage. This also touches the supervisor's one-page request.
- Table 4.5 runs from p. 55 to p. 61. The methodology-relevant content is the "Found by" and "Effect on the figures" columns. The rest can move to an appendix to help the shortening request, keeping one paragraph per category in the main text.
- Table 5.4 is "compiled by hand" and its "Methods" column counts only named methods. State the rule that makes the "Methods" count 1 for four of five classes in the table note, since Section 6.2 relies on it.
- Section 6.2 repeats each threat from earlier chapters. A three-column table (threat, evidence, status) could replace most of the seven pages.
- "similar precision" in the abstract: give both numbers (72.6 % and 75.2 %).
- Define "in-sample" once in the abstract and use "fitted" only where the repair history justifies it.
- Table 5.18 note: say the "Repaired extractor" row is fully post hoc and that only missed pairs were re-read in the second row.
