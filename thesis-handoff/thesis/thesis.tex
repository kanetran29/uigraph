\thesisabstract{%
A user interface (UI) transition graph represents an application's screens and the navigation
between them. For single-page applications, such graphs usually come from execution. Static
extraction avoids execution, but its losses have not been measured construct by construct.
\abspar
This thesis measures what static extraction recovers from Angular applications and why it loses
the rest. It proposes scope attribution, a dataflow analysis that computes the screens from which
each navigation site can fire.
\abspar
An extractor was applied to six open-source Angular applications and checked with five evaluation
methods. Every unrecovered navigation construct was classified.
\abspar
Every screen readable from route tables has a node. Only \CensusMustSites\ constructs yield
deterministic transitions under stated assumptions, largely because the study defines them strictly. Under the study's classification order, the largest loss
category is navigation outside components that routes render directly. Scope attribution
recovers a per-application median of \AttrRecoveredMedian\ of these constructs (\AttrRecoveredShare\ pooled, most of them in one application). Almost all become possible transitions, mostly
through mechanisms from earlier work. The added step, for services, guards and effects, places
\AblationFullAddedSites\ further cited source locations, \AblationFullAddedShare\ of all placed source locations.
\abspar
On a reference that applies the
analysis's own relations to one application's source, the added step trades precision for recall. A comparison with
Rima et al.'s tool gives no overall ranking: this extractor recalls as much or more at similar precision, except on one application, where it is far less precise. Both comparisons rest on small samples read by an artificial
intelligence model, and one subgroup was formed after reading. Mutation testing only sanity-checks
the mapping between source and witnesses.
\abspar
Route tables give static extraction a starting point, but attribution and target resolution remain
necessary. The main corpus results are in-sample because the extractor was developed on these
applications. The graph's benefit to users is not measured.
}

methodological choices and interpretation of the results are my own.

An AI coding assistant supported the implementation of the algorithms presented in this
thesis, including the extractor and evaluation code. I reviewed the implementation and remain
responsible for its correctness.

AI tools also helped identify candidate open-source projects and research papers. I assessed
their relevance and selected the projects and sources used in the study.

AI was used to review the implementation, evaluation and manuscript, and to identify possible
errors, inconsistencies and gaps in the arguments. I evaluated that feedback and revised the
text myself. The corrections made in response to reviews are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}.

AI-assisted readings of application source code also form part of the evaluation evidence,
including the Alfresco structural reference, the must-tier readings and the transition-pair
judgements described in Chapters~\ref{ch:evaluation} to~\ref{ch:discussion}. Their role and
limitations are stated there; they are not treated as independent human validation.

I take responsibility for the final text, implementation, results and conclusions.

\subsection*{Terms}


\begin{description}[nosep]
\item[Anchored edge] An edge stored once at its scope, $\top$ or $\mathit{subtree}(r)$, instead of
once per screen (Section~\ref{sec:anchored}).
\item[Attribution level] One of the nested configurations at which the ablation extracts the
corpus. The levels \emph{owned}, \emph{nesting}, \emph{rooted}, \emph{outlet} and \emph{full}
are nested, each adding mechanisms to the one before. \emph{Class} replaces the call graph of \emph{full} with a
per-class join (Section~\ref{sec:metrics} and Figure~\ref{fig:lattice}).
\item[Attribution loss] A lost navigation construct in one of the two attribution categories. It
has no owning component, is owned by a class that is not a component, such as a service or guard,
or lies in a component that no route renders directly (Table~\ref{tab:loss-rules}).
\item[Clean citation] A witness citation that the witness check reads and passes. Citations at
over-approximated sites, and those whose target the check cannot read, are neither clean nor
failures (Section~\ref{sec:res-soundness}).
\item[Demotion] Lowering a site from must to may because a condition of Table~\ref{tab:demotion}
holds (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item[Dynamic target, dynamic sink] A nonscreen node, a screen's \texttt{u\_}\,sink, for an
unresolved destination. Edges into it have modality unknown (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item[Modality] The confidence label of an edge: \emph{must} if it is claimed to fire for every
input on which the enclosing code executes, \emph{may} if it is possible but conditional, and
\emph{unknown} if it could not be determined (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item[Navigation construct] A \texttt{routerLink} binding in a template, or a call in class code to
\texttt{Router.navigate} or \texttt{Router.navigateByUrl}, outside tests. It is
\emph{in scope} unless it changes only the query string, targets a named outlet or names no
destination (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}).
\item[Outlet descent] Giving the component of route $r$, and the components it renders, the scope
$\mathit{subtree}(r)$, the screens of $r$ and its descendants (Section~\ref{sec:inputs}).
\item[Placed screen] A screen node, rather than an unplaced origin, as the source of an edge.
Recovery counts must and may edges from placed screens (Section~\ref{sec:res-accounting}).
\item[Red-team panel] An adversarial review, run with the declared AI assistant, in which several
reviewers read the graph and the source and each try to find errors
(Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}).
\item[Relative recall] The share of the real transition pairs in the union of two tools' pairs that
one tool finds (Section~\ref{sec:res-h2h}).
\item[Route-order assumption] Assumption A1: a catch-all in a separately registered route table is
matched after every route of the others (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item[Runtime route oracle] The evaluation method that loads each built application in a headless
browser and compares the addresses at which the Angular router mounts a component with the screens
of the graph (Table~\ref{tab:methods} and Section~\ref{sec:res-probe}).
\item[Scope] The set of screens from which a site can fire. The extractor emits edges from the
\emph{computed scope}, whereas the \emph{true scope} is a property of the application that this
thesis does not establish (Section~\ref{sec:scope}).
\item[Screen] A graph node for a route that renders a component, identified by the concatenated
paths of the route and its ancestors (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item[Screen without a node] A leaf address at which the runtime route oracle finds a mounted component
but the graph has no node (Section~\ref{sec:res-probe}).
\item[Shell] The components from the root to the primary router outlet. They and everything they
render get the scope $\top$ of all screens (Section~\ref{sec:inputs}).
\item[Site, navigation site] A source location from which a navigation can fire. Scope
attribution gives each site a scope (Section~\ref{sec:scope}). The construct accounting counts
one site per navigation construct (Section~\ref{sec:res-accounting}). The ablation counts every
location that a witness cites, so one construct can contribute more than one site there
(Section~\ref{sec:res-ablation}).
\item[Static reader] A tool that enumerates screens from the route configuration in the source:
the extractor or the independent route parser (Section~\ref{sec:res-baseline}).
\item[Structural reference] The (site, screen) pairs read from an application's source under the
analysis's own relations, independently of its implementation, so agreement with it does not
establish the true scope (Section~\ref{sec:scope}).
\item[Unplaced origin] A nonscreen node, prefixed \texttt{o\_}, for a component whose sites could
not be assigned to a screen. Its edges are labelled may, or unknown when they reach a dynamic target (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item[Witness, witness citation] The file and line of the construct that generates an edge, or an
observation identifier. Other constructs producing that edge become merged witnesses. Witness
citations count every cited location, repeats included (Sections~\ref{sec:model}
and~\ref{sec:res-soundness}).
\item[Witness check] The evaluation method that re-parses the witness of every must edge at the
cited line, compares the construct's kind and target with the edge, and tests the destination
against the independent route set (Table~\ref{tab:methods}).
\end{description}
