\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\vspace{-1.5em}
A user-interface (UI) transition graph represents application screens and navigation between them.
Such graphs are usually obtained by executing single-page applications. This thesis measures static
extraction on six open-source Angular applications using five evaluation methods and a construct-level
classification of unrecovered navigation. It proposes scope attribution, a dataflow analysis of the
screens from which a navigation site may fire.

Under the study's demotion rules and execution assumptions, \CensusMustSites{} constructs yield
\RouteOrderMust{} must edges. Of these, \CensusMustKdash{} come from one toolbar whose scope has not
been validated at run time; \RouteOrderMustWithout{} remain without the route-order assumption.
The small must tier largely reflects the classification rules rather than an inherent limit of
static analysis.

Scope attribution recovers \AttrRecoveredShare{} of the constructs assigned to attribution loss,
or \AttrRecoveredShareNoMifos{} without the largest application. Most recovered constructs yield
possible transitions through mechanisms from earlier work. The added step places navigation in services, guards
and effects, contributing \AblationFullAddedSites{} sites (\AblationFullAddedShare{}) above outlet
attribution. Its member-level call graph generalises Rima et al.'s bounded router-service tracing
and adds \AblationCallGraphNetSites{} sites (\AblationCallGraphNetShare{}) over a per-class join.

On the \GTAlfSites{} determined sites of an AI-built Alfresco reference, full attribution increases
recall from \GTAlfClassRecall{} to \GTAlfFullRecall{} while reducing precision from
\GTAlfClassPrecision{} to \GTAlfFullPrecision{}. The reference applies the analysis's own relations,
so agreement does not establish true runtime scope. A comparison with Rima et al.'s tool also shows
a coverage--precision trade-off, without an overall ranking. It uses small AI-read samples; its
five-application subgroup was selected after inspecting the results. This extractor's estimated
precision on the largest application is \HHMifosOursPrecision{}. Mutation testing provides only a
conditional sanity check of source-to-witness mapping.

Route tables provide a starting point for static extraction, but source attribution and target
resolution remain necessary. The main corpus results are in-sample because the extractor was
developed on these applications. Consumer benefit is not measured.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} static analysis; UI transition graph; single-page
application; Angular; dataflow analysis; program analysis; software testing

\chapter*{Use of AI tools}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Use of AI tools}

I wrote the text of this thesis and made the revisions myself. Artificial intelligence (AI)
tools were used to support implementation, the search for relevant projects and papers, and
review. I assessed the suggestions and decided which changes to make. The research questions,
methodological choices and interpretation of the results are my own.

An AI coding assistant supported the implementation of the algorithms presented in this
thesis, including the extractor and evaluation code. I reviewed the implementation and remain
responsible for its correctness.

AI tools also helped identify candidate open-source projects and research papers. I assessed
their relevance and selected the projects and sources used in the study.

AI was used to review the implementation, evaluation and manuscript, and to identify possible
errors, inconsistencies and gaps in the arguments. I evaluated that feedback and revised the
text myself. The corrections made in response to reviews are recorded in
Section~\ref{app:repairs}.

AI-assisted readings of application source code also form part of the evaluation evidence,
including the Alfresco structural reference, the must-tier readings and the transition-pair
judgements described in Chapters~\ref{ch:evaluation} to~\ref{ch:discussion}. Their role and
limitations are stated there; they are not treated as independent human validation.

I take responsibility for the final text, implementation, results and conclusions.
